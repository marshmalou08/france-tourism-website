Fête des Lumières

The Fête des Lumières — the Festival of Lights — is Lyon's most celebrated annual event and one of the most spectacular public art experiences in the world. Held each year over four nights in early December, the festival transforms the entire city of Lyon into an open-air gallery of light, with dozens of large-scale illuminated installations created by artists from France and around the world adorning buildings, squares, bridges, riverbanks, and parks.

The festival has its roots in a centuries-old tradition of gratitude. On December 8, 1852, the citizens of Lyon placed lit candles on their windowsills to give thanks to the Virgin Mary for protecting their city from a plague epidemic in the 17th century. This annual gesture of thousands of glowing windows evolved over time into the spectacular modern festival that now draws nearly two million visitors each year — making it one of the largest and most attended public events in all of France.

During the festival, Lyon's streets are filled with wonder — fountains of light dance across cathedral facades, entire bridges ripple with color, and unexpected installations appear in intimate courtyards and along the riverside quays. The festival is entirely free to attend, meaning that on its four magical nights, all of Lyon becomes a shared stage. The Fourvière hill blazes with projections, the Vieux Lyon quays shimmer with reflections in the Saône, and the sound of music fills the crisp December air. Attending the Fête des Lumières is an experience that stays with every visitor for a lifetime.
