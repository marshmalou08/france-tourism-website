NORMANDY

Normandy, in northwestern France, is a region of extraordinary contrasts — dramatic chalk cliffs and apple orchards, medieval abbeys and D-Day beaches, half-timbered market towns and the ethereal silhouette of Mont Saint-Michel rising from the sea. Normandy's landscapes are lush and green, shaped by the soft Atlantic rains that nurture its famous dairy farms, cider orchards, and fishing ports. At once deeply peaceful and profoundly historic, it is a region that invites slow, contemplative travel — walking the clifftop paths of Étretat, sitting quietly in the D-Day cemeteries, or simply watching the tides circle Mont Saint-Michel at dawn.

Mont Saint-Michel is Normandy's crowning glory and one of France's most visited landmarks. This medieval abbey commune, rising dramatically from a tidal island at the boundary of Normandy and Brittany, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the world's most recognizable architectural silhouettes. The abbey, perched at the summit of the rocky island, has been a place of Christian pilgrimage since the 8th century. At high tide, the sea surrounds the island entirely; at low tide, visitors can walk the sand flats that surround it. The narrow streets of the island village, packed with medieval houses, lead visitors upward through the ramparts to the magnificent abbey church at the top.

Normandy is also a land of profound historical memory. The D-Day beaches of June 6, 1944 — Utah, Omaha, Gold, Juno, and Sword — stretch along the Calvados coast, and the cemeteries, memorials, and museums that commemorate the Allied landings draw visitors from across the world. The American Cemetery at Colleville-sur-Mer, overlooking Omaha Beach, with its rows of white marble crosses, is among the most moving sites in all of Europe. The Normandy landscape, with its apple orchards, half-timbered farmhouses, and gray skies, has an atmosphere all its own — somewhere between melancholy and beauty.

Norman Cuisine

Camembert de Normandie

The world's most famous soft cheese hails from the village of Camembert in the Orne department. Made from raw Normandy milk, true Camembert de Normandie has a rich, runny interior and a white rind with a distinctively earthy, mushroomy aroma.

Moules Marinière

Normandy's productive coastline yields excellent mussels, prepared here in the classic French style — steamed in white wine, shallots, butter, and parsley. Simple, fragrant, and deeply satisfying when served with a basket of crusty bread and a glass of local cider.

Tarte Normande

A classic Norman dessert tart made with a buttery pastry shell filled with sliced apples, eggs, cream, and Calvados apple brandy. The combination of sweet apples, rich cream, and fragrant Calvados is the very essence of Norman baking tradition.

Calvados

Normandy's most famous spirit — an apple brandy distilled from the cider apples that grow in the region's orchards. Well-aged Calvados is complex and aromatic, traditionally sipped between courses as a digestif known as the "trou normand."
