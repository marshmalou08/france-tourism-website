Louvre Museum

The Louvre Museum is the world's largest and most visited art museum, located in the heart of Paris on the banks of the River Seine. Originally built as a medieval fortress in the 12th century, it was transformed into a royal palace before becoming a public museum during the French Revolution in 1793.

The museum is home to more than 35,000 works of art spanning thousands of years of human history, from ancient civilizations to the mid-19th century. Among its most celebrated treasures are Leonardo da Vinci's Mona Lisa, the Venus de Milo, and the Winged Victory of Samothrace.

The iconic glass pyramid entrance, designed by architect I. M. Pei and completed in 1989, has become a landmark in its own right. Visitors can explore eight curatorial departments covering Egyptian antiquities, Greek and Roman artifacts, Islamic art, sculptures, decorative arts, paintings, prints, and drawings. A visit to the Louvre is an immersive journey through the breadth of human artistic achievement.
