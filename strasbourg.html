STRASBOURG

Strasbourg, the capital of the Alsace region in northeastern France, is a city of extraordinary charm where French and German cultures blend seamlessly. Its fairytale medieval old town — La Petite France — with its timber-framed houses and winding canals is one of France's most photographed neighborhoods. Strasbourg is home to a magnificent Gothic cathedral, one of the tallest in the world, and serves as the seat of the European Parliament. The city is also world-famous for hosting one of Europe's oldest and most magical Christmas markets, transforming its historic streets into a winter wonderland every December.

The historic heart of Strasbourg — the Grande Île — was the first entire city center to be classified as a UNESCO World Heritage Site. At its center stands the Cathédrale Notre-Dame de Strasbourg, whose intricate pink sandstone facade and soaring single tower dominate the Alsatian skyline. The cathedral's astronomical clock, dating from the 16th century, is a marvel of medieval engineering that still performs its daily show. Surrounding the cathedral, a maze of streets leads to the iconic quarter of La Petite France, where restored half-timbered houses lean over reflective canals crossed by covered bridges.

Strasbourg's Alsatian cuisine is a world unto itself — a hearty, flavorful blend of French finesse and German robustness. The city's winstubs (traditional wine taverns) serve classics like choucroute garnie (sauerkraut with meats), flammekueche (Alsatian tarte flambée), and baeckeoffe stew, all accompanied by excellent Alsatian wines. Whether exploring its European institutions, admiring its Gothic architecture, or savoring its unique cuisine, Strasbourg offers a one-of-a-kind experience found nowhere else in France.

Alsatian Gastronomy

Tarte Flambée

Alsace's answer to pizza — a thin, crispy flatbread spread with crème fraîche, onions, and lardons, baked in a wood-fired oven. Light, fragrant, and absolutely delicious with a glass of Alsatian Riesling.

Choucroute Garnie

The quintessential Alsatian dish — a generous platter of sauerkraut slow-cooked in Riesling wine and topped with an assortment of pork cuts, sausages, and smoked meats. Hearty, warming, and deeply satisfying.

Kougelhopf

A traditional Alsatian brioche cake baked in a distinctive fluted ring mold, studded with raisins and almonds, and dusted with powdered sugar. A beloved breakfast treat and festive specialty unique to Alsace.

Baeckeoffe

A traditional slow-baked casserole of marinated lamb, beef, and pork with potatoes and vegetables, sealed in a terracotta dish with Riesling. Historically prepared on laundry day and left to cook all morning.
