PROVENCE

Provence is a region in southeastern France that seems designed by nature to captivate the senses. Famous for its vast purple lavender fields, rolling vineyards, olive groves, and the warm golden light that inspired generations of painters from Cézanne to Van Gogh, Provence offers a pastoral beauty found nowhere else in France. Its Roman heritage, medieval hilltop villages, vibrant weekly markets, and exquisite cuisine rooted in olive oil, herbs, and sun-ripened produce make it one of the most cherished and sought-after regions in all of Europe.

The landscapes of Provence are endlessly varied and endlessly beautiful. In June and July, the lavender fields of the Luberon and the Valensole plateau paint entire hillsides in brilliant purple — a sight that has become one of France's most iconic images. The famous Gorges du Verdon, cut deep into limestone plateaus by the brilliant turquoise Verdon River, offers some of the most dramatic scenery in Europe. The Camargue, the vast wetlands delta of the Rhône at Provence's southern tip, is home to wild white horses, pink flamingos, and black bulls.

Provence is dotted with some of France's most enchanting villages. Gordes, Les Baux-de-Provence, Roussillon with its ochre-colored cliffs, and Moustiers-Sainte-Marie perched above a waterfall all draw visitors who lose themselves in their narrow lanes and terracotta-roofed houses. The city of Aix-en-Provence — elegant, fountain-filled, and alive with café culture — was the birthplace of Paul Cézanne and remains one of the most pleasant towns in France. And Avignon, home to the magnificent Palais des Papes, preserves one of the most complete medieval urban environments in Europe.

Provençal Cuisine

Ratatouille

Provence's most famous vegetable dish — a slow-cooked stew of tomatoes, zucchini, eggplant, peppers, and aromatic herbs. Made with the finest summer produce and a generous pour of local olive oil, it captures the essence of Provençal cooking.

Bouillabaisse

The iconic fish stew of Marseille, made with a saffron-scented broth and an assortment of fresh Mediterranean fish and shellfish. Traditionally served in two courses — the broth first with rouille and croutons, then the fish separately.

Tapenade

A Provençal purée of black or green olives blended with capers, anchovies, and olive oil. Intensely savory and fragrant with the flavors of the Mediterranean, it is spread on toast as an appetizer or used to flavor meat and vegetables.

Calisson d'Aix

The jewel of Aix-en-Provence's confectionery tradition — a diamond-shaped sweet made from ground almonds and candied melon, topped with a thin layer of white royal icing. A delicate, fragrant treat with a history stretching back to the 15th century.
