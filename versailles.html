VERSAILLES

Versailles, located just 20 kilometers southwest of Paris, is home to one of the most magnificent royal palaces in the world. The Palace of Versailles served as the political center of France and the official residence of the French Kings from Louis XIV to Louis XVI. Built over decades in the 17th and 18th centuries, the palace and its spectacular formal gardens represent the pinnacle of French baroque architecture and grandeur. Today, recognized as a UNESCO World Heritage Site, Versailles draws millions of visitors who come to marvel at the Hall of Mirrors, the royal apartments, and the vast sculpted gardens that stretch beyond the horizon.

The Palace of Versailles is an extraordinary monument to royal ambition and artistic mastery. Commissioned by the Sun King, Louis XIV, the palace began as a modest hunting lodge before being transformed into an immense complex encompassing over 700 rooms, including the breathtaking Hall of Mirrors — a 73-meter gallery lined with 357 mirrors reflecting the light of 20,000 candles. The palace's interiors are adorned with precious artworks, gilded furnishings, and painted ceilings that celebrate the power and glory of the French monarchy.

Beyond the palace walls, the Gardens of Versailles extend over 800 hectares, featuring symmetrical parterres, ornamental fountains, canals, and woodland groves. Designed by landscape architect André Le Nôtre, the gardens represent the French formal garden style at its most refined. On weekends from spring to autumn, the famous Grandes Eaux Musicales fountain shows bring the gardens to life with choreographed water displays set to baroque music. A visit to Versailles is an unforgettable immersion in the splendor of France's royal heritage.
