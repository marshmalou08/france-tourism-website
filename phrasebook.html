French Phrasebook

A few French phrases go a long way — the locals will appreciate every effort.

Speaking a Little French

French people genuinely appreciate when visitors make an effort to speak their language, even imperfectly. Beginning a conversation with "Bonjour" and ending with "Merci" will warm most interactions considerably. The phrases below are the most useful ones for everyday travel in France — from ordering at a café to asking for directions, checking in to a hotel, or simply being polite.

Essential Greetings

Bonjour

bon-ZHOOR
Hello / Good morning. The most important word in France — always greet shopkeepers, waiters, and strangers with "Bonjour" before speaking to them.

Bonsoir

bon-SWAHR
Good evening. Used from late afternoon onwards in place of "Bonjour."

Au revoir

oh ruh-VWAHR
Goodbye. Always say this when leaving a shop or restaurant — it is considered impolite to leave without it.

Merci (beaucoup)

mair-SEE (boh-KOO)
Thank you (very much). Simple and essential — used constantly throughout French daily life.

S'il vous plaît

seel voo PLAY
Please. Used when ordering, asking for something, or getting someone's attention — "Excusez-moi, s'il vous plaît" to politely attract a waiter.

Excusez-moi

ex-KOO-zay MWAH
Excuse me. Used to get attention, to pass through a crowd, or to apologize for a minor inconvenience.

At the Café & Restaurant

Une table pour deux, s'il vous plaît

oon TAH-bluh poor duh
A table for two, please. Replace "deux" (two) with "un" (one), "trois" (three), or "quatre" (four) as needed.

La carte, s'il vous plaît

lah KART
The menu, please. "La carte" is the full menu; "le menu" in France often refers to a fixed-price set meal.

Je voudrais...

zhuh voo-DRAY
I would like... The polite standard way to order anything — followed by what you want: "Je voudrais un café, s'il vous plaît."

L'addition, s'il vous plaît

lah-dee-SYOHN
The bill, please. Waiters in France will not bring the bill until you ask — it is considered polite to let guests linger at the table.

C'était délicieux !

say-tay day-lee-SYUH
It was delicious! A phrase that will delight any French chef or host — using it at the end of a meal is always appreciated.

Sans gluten / végétarien

sahn gloo-TEN / vay-zhay-tah-RYEH
Gluten-free / vegetarian. Useful for dietary requirements — "Je suis végétarien(ne)" means "I am vegetarian."

Getting Around

Où est... ?

oo ay
Where is...? The most useful question for a traveler: "Où est la gare?" (Where is the station?), "Où est le musée?" (Where is the museum?)

Je suis perdu(e)

zhuh swee pair-DOO
I am lost. A phrase that often generates helpful responses from locals — the French are generally very willing to assist lost tourists.

Un billet pour..., s'il vous plaît

uh bee-YAY poor
A ticket to..., please. Essential at train stations, metro stops, and tourist attractions.

Pouvez-vous répéter, s'il vous plaît ?

poo-VAY voo ray-pay-TAY
Could you repeat that, please? Do not hesitate to ask — speaking slowly and clearly is entirely normal when communicating with non-native speakers.

Now You're Ready

With a few French phrases in your pocket, you're prepared for a wonderful time in France. Bon voyage!
